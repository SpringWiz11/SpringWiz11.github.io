  1. arXiv
    Geometric Function Atlas: Certified Computing for Geometric Function Theory in Python
    Kishan Gurumurthy, Pushparaj Devadiga, Prasanna Devadiga, and 2 more authors
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.07969, 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    An open-source Python package for the sharp extremal problems of geometric function theory, organized around a catalogue of thirty-nine Ma-Minda starlike generators. A three-tier verification approach – a floating-point grid screen, an exact sufficient condition decided in rational arithmetic, and a certified interval enclosure – backs every result with an evidence level and an artifact identifier. The distribution ships three hundred and six coefficient certificates and seven hundred and two directed inclusion radii. Released under the MIT license on PyPI.

  1. TMLR
    Federated Spectral Graph Transformers Meet Neural Ordinary Differential Equations for Non-IID Graphs
    Kishan Gurumurthy, Himanshu Pal, and Charu Sharma
    Transactions on Machine Learning Research, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Centralizing large volumes of real-world graph data for GNN training is often impractical due to privacy concerns. We propose Fed-GNODEFormer, which couples spectral GNNs with neural ordinary differential equations to handle non-IID data distributions in federated settings. The method delivers significant improvements on non-IID heterophilic graphs while remaining competitive on homophilic datasets, with applications to social network analysis, recommendation, and fraud detection.

  2. TMLR
    Universal Differential Equations for Stable Multi-Step Volatility Time Series Forecasting
    Prasanna Devadiga, Kishan Gurumurthy, and Kshitij Mohan
    Transactions on Machine Learning Research, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML

    We present a systematic study of Universal Differential Equations for financial volatility forecasting, a domain marked by regime shifts, heavy tails, and jump discontinuities. UDEs extend Neural ODEs by embedding mechanistic structure within learned dynamics, parameterizing coefficients of a partially known differential equation rather than learning the system purely from data. Across market regimes our variants outperform both continuous-time baselines and discrete-time models, achieving higher accuracy and greater long-horizon stability while remaining interpretable.

  3. FIRE
    EventTempEx: A Tool for Interpreting Event-Anchored Temporal Expressions
    Niharika Lal, Pawan Kumar Rajpoot, Kishan Gurumurthy, and 1 more author
    In Proceedings of the 17th Annual Meeting of the Forum for Information Retrieval Evaluation (FIRE), 2025
    Abs Bib Code

    Temporal expressions that anchor to events rather than to the calendar resist standard normalization. We classify temporal references into standard, event-contextual, complex, and historic types using a fine-tuned DeBERTa NER model, combining a Duckling parser for standard references with semantic-search backends for event-contextual ones.

  5. CLEF
    TIFIN at CheckThat! 2025: Reasoning-Guided Claim Normalization for Noisy Multilingual Social Media Posts
    Manan Sharma, Arya Suneesh, Manish Jain, and 7 more authors
    In CLEF 2025 Working Notes, CheckThat! Lab, 2025
    Awarded Abs arXiv Bib HTML

    3rd place, English leaderboard

    Transforming noisy social media posts into clear, verifiable statements across 20 languages. Systematic 5W1H decomposition enables robust cross-lingual transfer despite training exclusively on English data, using a LoRA fine-tuned Qwen3-14B with retrieval-augmented few-shot prompting and token-level recall filtering. Ranked 3rd on the English leaderboard and 4th on Dutch and Punjabi.

  6. CLEF
    TIFIN at CheckThat! 2025: X-VERIFY – Multi-lingual NLI-based Fact Checking with Condensed Evidence
    Bharatdeep Hazarika, Prasanna Devadiga, Pawan Kumar Rajpoot, and 7 more authors
    In CLEF 2025 Working Notes, CheckThat! Lab, 2025
    Awarded Abs Bib HTML

    3rd place, English and Arabic tracks

    Verifying numerical claims as True, False, or Conflicting from retrieved evidence. The pipeline combines optimized evidence selection, LLM-based summarization with IBM Granite 3.3 8B, and a LoRA fine-tuned DeBERTa-large classifier. Multilingual augmentation lifts macro-F1 from 0.5815 to 0.6859, a 17.95% relative gain. Ranked 3rd in both English and Arabic.
